# Presentation types

The type defines the audience, the length, the sequence of blocks, and its own script and slide rules. The project only fills in the content.

## Product proposal for leadership

To present a direction to decision makers who already know the problem and want to see the experience. 5–8 minutes.

**Sequence:**
1. **Cover.** Title of the proposal. The greeting to the recipient is spoken, not on the slide.
2. **Problem**, 1–2 slides, quick:
   - a capture of the current experience in a real store, with 2–3 numbered annotations pinned to points of the capture (the number appears on the capture and before each sentence);
   - a data point showing what is gained by solving it (e.g. conversion of a specific search versus a generic one), as bars, with one reading sentence.
3. **Premises**, 2–4, one slide each. Each slide has a title, one sentence and an animated illustration built with the prototype's real components, showing the premise happen (before and after, with a pointer). The animation is triggered by the narration, not looped.
4. **Cases in the prototype**, 3–4, in increasing effort for the user: from the way they already search today to the most elaborate request. The first case requires learning nothing new. Each case has a cover: grey background, "Case N of M" in a corner, and what the user sends ("One word", "Link", "Photo and sentence", "Document"), never a difficulty level.
5. **Proposal**, 1 slide: a synthesis sentence as the title and 3 cards with what the user gains. Each card title is a verb with an implicit subject (e.g. "Describes what they need however they prefer"), without marketing tone. Cards enter one per spoken line.
6. **Back cover.** Only the brand. Over it: the concrete next step (and who it will be aligned with) and the goodbye.

**Script:**
- Little time on the problem, more on the cases.
- Separate what exists today from what is proposed.
- Mention what happens behind the scenes (e.g. several agents) in the right premise, without implementation detail.
- No question to the recipient at the end.

**Slides:**
- Brand style: no grey fragment in titles, no slide counter, no all caps, main content out of the bottom strip.
- Breathing room between elements and no awkward line breaks.

## Adding a type

A new type uses the same shape: audience, length, sequence, and its own script and slide rules.
